Buyer Profile & Transaction Strategy
Who Is Most Likely to Buy, and How We Create Competition

Market Context

The buyer for this building is not buying a repositioning. Rents are already at or near the top of the Sherman Oaks range for this unit type, the interiors are done, and the capital items a buyer normally holds money back for are behind it. What they are buying is a rule set: a Los Angeles apartment building where the annual increase is a published formula, turnover resets to market, and the operating statement has five lines on it.

That pool is smaller than the pool for a twenty unit RSO building and it moves faster. The competing Sherman Oaks listing, five townhomes on Stern Avenue, is asking $639,000 a unit and has been exposed for 163 days through multiple price reductions without trading, which tells us where the ceiling is and what happens above it.

Days on market across the three closed sales: 17 days on 13430 Victory Blvd, which closed 1.7% above its ask; 13 days on 10833-10835 Camarillo St, which closed 9.2% below its ask; and zero on 4609 Kester Ave, which never reached the MLS as a listing and traded off market. The three active listings carried here have been out 163, 147 and 72 days, and the two asking the most per unit are the two still sitting.

Positioning Thesis

We position the building on what a buyer owns on day one rather than on what they could do to it. Seven large townhome units in prime Sherman Oaks, one delivered vacant, in-unit laundry and a private garage space behind a gate, no rent board, and a landlord expense load limited to water, power in the common areas and refuse. The rent upside is real but modest, and we would rather be believed on a 5% number than doubted on a 20% one.

15146 Moorpark St is a seven unit apartment building in the heart of Sherman Oaks, built in 2005 and delivered on a certificate of occupancy issued 23 February 2007. The units are two story townhomes, six two-bedroom and one three-bedroom, laid out across 10,780 building square feet on a 7,804 square foot lot, over a gated subterranean garage. Every unit has its own washer and dryer, a fireplace, central heat and air, a private balcony, and its own gas and electric meter. Tenants share a roof deck with a view across Ventura Boulevard.

The certificate of occupancy date is what separates this building from most of what trades in the Valley. The Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance reaches buildings occupied before 1 October 1978, and ZIMAS returns RSO: No for this parcel. Annual increases follow AB 1482, the state Tenant Protection Act, at 5% plus regional CPI to a 10% ceiling, and turnover resets to market.

Six of the seven units are occupied on month to month tenancies at $2,975 to $3,575. The seventh is vacant and delivers empty. Ownership's own 2026 statements show a landlord cost structure limited to water, common-area power, refuse, insurance and routine service, because every unit is separately metered and every unit does its own laundry.

Location Overview
One Block North of Ventura Boulevard in Sherman Oaks

The property sits on Moorpark Street one block north of Ventura Boulevard, roughly a tenth of a mile from the Sherman Oaks Galleria at 15301 Ventura Blvd and a short walk from Whole Foods, Trader Joe's and Westfield Fashion Square. ZIMAS places the parcel inside a Transit Priority Area, within a half mile of a major transit stop under AB 2097, in Transit Oriented Communities Tier 3, and in the state's highest resource tier for opportunity. The 101 is four minutes north and the 405 four minutes west.

The largest thing happening to this location is not on the block. On 22 January 2026 the Metro Board selected the Locally Preferred Alternative for the Sepulveda Transit Corridor, a fully underground heavy rail line running from the Van Nuys Metrolink station under the Santa Monica Mountains to the Westside, with a Ventura Boulevard station in Sherman Oaks. Metro estimates a 20 minute one seat ride from the Valley to UCLA and Westwood on a route that today takes an hour in traffic. The project carries a $24.2B budget and the initial operating segment is targeted for 2033 to 2035. It is in environmental review, not under construction, so nothing in this valuation assumes it. A Sherman Oaks rental one block from the boulevard that station will sit on is nonetheless a different asset in 2035 than it is today.

The retail around it is turning over in the buyer's favor. Hapa Haus is taking 7,983 square feet at the Sherman Oaks Galleria, with 69 indoor seats, a covered patio and five golf simulators, targeting a November 2026 opening. Yama Sushi Marketplace is building out roughly 7,200 square feet at 15300 Ventura Blvd. Burger She Wrote opened its second location at 14245 Ventura Blvd in March 2026, and Aromas Indian Cuisine is taking the former Billa space at 14350 Ventura Blvd. All four sit within about half a mile of the property.

New housing is arriving too, and most of it is the kind that sets a rent ceiling rather than competing for this tenant. Via Avanti at 4827 N Sepulveda Blvd is a 325 unit mixed-use complex with ground-floor retail near the Galleria that finished wrapping at the end of 2025. IMT Residential has been building on the former Sunkist Growers headquarters site at 14130 Riverside Drive since it bought the 8.5 acres in 2013: Citrus Commons put 249 apartments and a Trader Joe's on the surface parking, and IMT has now filed to convert the 125,000 square foot Brutalist headquarters building itself into 95 residences. A 75 unit affordable building is proposed at 14318 W Dickens St, one block south of Ventura. Institutional owners do not commit twice to a block they expect to soften, and new institutional product asks institutional rents. A 2005 townhome with its own washer and dryer and a private garage space rents under it without feeling like it.

Sherman Oaks RSO pricing was reported in January 2026 to have held flat per unit through 2025 while cap rates expanded and gross rent multipliers compressed, and the gap between RSO and AB 1482 assets is expected to widen. That is the whole thesis for this building in one sentence. Buyers are paying up for predictable rent growth and a clean operating story, and they are discounting everything that comes with a rent board.
